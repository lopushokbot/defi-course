Understanding Perpetual DEXs: The Decentralised Trading Revolution

Perpetual futures are derivative contracts with no expiration date, allowing positions to be held indefinitely — and anyone can provide liquidity.

What Are Perpetual DEXs?

Perpetual futures are derivative contracts with no expiration date, allowing positions to be held indefinitely.

  • Traders can use substantial leverage (e.g., 10x long or short ETH/USDC) to amplify returns.
  • Traditional CeFi exchanges like Binance and Bybit rely on centralised market makers for liquidity.
  • In the DeFi ecosystem, protocols like GMX, Hyperliquid, and Jupiter democratise market making — anyone can provide liquidity.
  • Perpetual DEXs let you have custody of your assets, as you trade from your wallet.

How Yield Is Generated

1

Fee Collection

Traders pay fees for opening/closing positions and funding rates (paid periodically between longs and shorts).

2

LP Counterparty Role

Liquidity providers (LPs) collectively act as the counterparty to all trader positions.

3

PnL Distribution

If traders lose, LPs gain (collecting liquidations + net PnL). If traders win, LPs cover their profits (absorbing losses).

Many protocols also distribute native tokens as additional rewards to LPs, boosting effective yields during incentive periods.

A Practical Example

1

Initial LP Deposit

You provide $10,000 USDT as liquidity on GMX, joining the collective counterparty pool.

2

Trader Activity

A trader opens a 10x long ETH position and ultimately loses $100 in PnL.

3

Fee Generation

Goes to liquidity pool: Opening fee (~$3), Closing fee (~$3), Funding rate payments, The trader's $100 loss.

📉 If ETH Drops

Trader may be liquidated — their collateral is distributed to LPs (you earn).

📈 If ETH Rises

Trader may profit — their gains come from the LP pool (you lose a portion).

Key insight: You always earn trading fees regardless of market direction or trader outcomes.
