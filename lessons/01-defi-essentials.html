What is DeFi?

Decentralised Finance (DeFi) represents a fundamental shift in how financial services operate — built on smart contracts rather than traditional financial institutions, such as brokers.

Unlike traditional finance with its middlemen, DeFi creates direct connections between participants. There's no bank sitting in the middle, holding your funds, deciding whether to approve your transaction, or taking a cut of every operation.

Smart contracts can perform various financial functions — this variability enables different strategies for generating yields in the DeFi space. In the following lessons, you'll learn practical, real-world strategies to implement.

The transparency and self-custody aspects of DeFi represent its most revolutionary features, giving users unprecedented control over their financial activities.

Centralised vs DeFi: The ETH to USDC Exchange

To understand the practical difference between CeFi and DeFi, let's compare how a simple token swap works on each.

🏢 Centralised Exchange (CEX)

  1. Send ETH from your wallet to Binance (commission)
  2. Create sell order on exchange
  3. Receive USDC after sale
  4. Transfer USDC back to personal wallet (commission)

Your funds are held by the exchange throughout the process.

🔄 Decentralised Exchange (DEX)

  1. Connect wallet directly to Uniswap
  2. Interact with swap smart contract (commission)
  3. ETH is exchanged for USDC in one transaction

You maintain custody of your assets throughout.

DeFi vs CeFi: The Core Differences

Here's a side-by-side comparison of the key differences between centralised finance and decentralised finance.

Feature CeFi (Binance, Coinbase…) DeFi (Aave, Uniswap…)
Custody Exchange holds your funds You hold your wallet keys
Transparency Trust in company, Audits On-chain, transparent smart contracts
Access KYC / limits No KYC
Risks Exchange bankruptcy, hacks Smart contract hacks

Real-World DeFi Yield Opportunities

DeFi offers multiple ways to generate returns on your crypto assets. Here are the four primary strategies you'll learn in this course.

🏦

1. Lending Markets

Similar to traditional bank deposits, but with higher returns.

  • Deposit stablecoins like USDT/USDC into protocols like Aave or Compound
  • Earn 5–15% interest on your deposits
  • Others borrow at 5–7% rates, with the difference creating protocol reserves
🔄

2. Liquidity Provision (DEX/AMM)

Earn fees by supporting trading pairs on decentralised exchanges.

  • Deposit balanced pairs (e.g., 1,000 USDT + 1,000 USDC) into a liquidity pool
  • Earn a percentage of each trade that uses your liquidity
  • Yield often exceeds 10% depending on trading volume
📈

3. Liquidity Against Traders (Perpetuals)

Higher risk, higher reward option that benefits from trader liquidations.

  • Provide USDT as liquidity on perpetual DEXs like GMX or Hyperliquid
  • Your balance grows when leveraged traders get liquidated
  • Average yields can reach ~20%
⛓

4. Staking

Support blockchain networks while earning native token rewards.

  • Stake tokens like SOL, ETH, or ATOM to secure their networks
  • Earn network rewards, typically ~7% annually in SOL as an example
  • Lower risk but subject to token price volatility
